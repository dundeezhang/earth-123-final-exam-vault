# Module 07 - Water Balance Calculations

Up: [[00 - EARTH 123 Final Exam Home]]

**Central question:** How can measured water inputs, outputs, storage, and surface area be combined consistently to determine a system's change in water volume or elevation?

## Highest-Yield Exam Points

1. A water balance is an accounting of all water inputs and outputs for a defined system; their difference is the change in storage, $\Delta S$.
2. For the lake model, inputs are precipitation $P$, surface-water inflow $Q_{\text{in}}$, and groundwater inflow $G_{\text{in}}$.
3. Outputs are evapotranspiration $ET$, surface-water outflow $Q_{\text{out}}$, and groundwater outflow $G_{\text{out}}$.
4. The full balance is $\Delta S=(P+Q_{\text{in}}+G_{\text{in}})-(ET+Q_{\text{out}}+G_{\text{out}})$.
5. Never add a depth rate such as mm/day directly to a volume rate such as m$^3$/day. Convert depths to metres, then multiply by system area.
6. A positive $\Delta S$ means storage increased; a negative $\Delta S$ means storage decreased.
7. Equal inflow and outflow terms cancel, including $G_{\text{in}}=G_{\text{out}}$ in several course examples.
8. At steady state, $\Delta S=0$, so the balance can be rearranged to solve an unknown flux such as $Q_{\text{in}}$.
9. Lake-surface elevation change is the volume change divided by lake area: $\Delta h=\Delta S/A$.
10. Time units must also match. For example, multiply m$^3$/s by $3{,}600$ to obtain m$^3$/hr before combining hourly rates.
11. The same balance framework applies across scales, from plants and lysimeters to lakes, watersheds, oceans, and Earth.
12. For the course's watershed knowledge check, there is no surface-water inflow across the topographic watershed boundary; the measured surface-water term is outflow.

## Equations and Quantitative Skills

$$
\Delta S=(P+Q_{\text{in}}+G_{\text{in}})-(ET+Q_{\text{out}}+G_{\text{out}})
$$

$$
V_{\text{depth flux}}=(\text{depth})(A)
$$

$$
\Delta h=\frac{\Delta S}{A}
$$

- Convert $1\ \text{mm}=0.001\ \text{m}$ and $1\ \text{km}^2=1{,}000{,}000\ \text{m}^2$.
- Convert every term to one common volume-per-time unit before substitution.
- Average multiple precipitation measurements before converting the representative depth to volume.
- Use the sign of $\Delta S$ and $\Delta h$ to state increase or decrease explicitly.

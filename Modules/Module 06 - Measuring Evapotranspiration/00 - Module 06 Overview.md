## Highest-Yield Exam Points

1. **Potential evapotranspiration (ET)** is the maximum possible with unlimited water and unsaturated air; **actual ET** reflects current environmental conditions, so actual ET is always less than or equal to potential ET.
2. An evaporation pan estimates potential evaporation from water-depth loss over time; a lysimeter measures actual ET from a soil-and-vegetation sample.
3. Pan siting matters. Surface, sunken, and floating pans exchange heat and interact with wind, debris, runoff, and surrounding water differently.
4. A pan coefficient corrects measured pan loss for local pan type, location, month, and season. Apply the coefficient before dividing by elapsed time.
5. A weighing lysimeter infers ET from mass loss; a water balance lysimeter monitors water levels, inflows, and outflows.
6. In the energy-balance method, incoming radiation minus reflected shortwave and outgoing longwave radiation is allocated among evaporation, air warming, surface warming, and plant growth.
7. A lake water balance solves evaporation as the residual after accounting for precipitation, surface-water and groundwater flows, and change in storage.
8. Penman-Monteith estimates potential ET from atmospheric and surface data; empirical equations are locally calibrated and usually do not transfer reliably to unlike sites.
9. An evapotranspiration chamber captures vapour from a small plant and its covered surface. Subtracting a comparable bare-surface chamber measurement isolates plant transpiration.
10. Groundwater-fluctuation estimates require a shallow, plant-accessible water table and use nocturnal water-table recovery, the 24-hour level change, and effective specific yield, the drainable-water fraction that converts water-table movement to equivalent water depth.
11. Track signs and time bases carefully: in the section 6.3 example, the 24-hour water-table change $b$ is negative because the level is lower at the end of the interval.
12. Unit conversions drive the lysimeter and groundwater calculations: $1\ \mathrm{L}$ of water corresponds to $1\ \mathrm{kg}$, $1000\ \mathrm{L}=1\ \mathrm{m^3}$, and $1\ \mathrm{m}=1000\ \mathrm{mm}$.

## Equations and Quantitative Skills

### Pan measurements

$$
E_{pan}=\frac{\text{water-depth loss}}{\text{elapsed time}}
$$

$$
E_{adjusted}=\frac{(\text{water-depth loss})K_p}{\text{elapsed time}}
$$

### Lysimeters

$$
V=A d
$$

Determine water mass or volume lost, convert volume to cubic metres, solve $d=V/A$, convert depth to millimetres, and divide by elapsed time.

### Energy and water balances

$$
R-R_E-R_A=H_E+H_A+H_B+H_C
$$

$$
H_E=R-R_E-R_A \quad \text{(simplified course form)}
$$

$$
E=P+SW_{in}+GW_{in}-SW_{out}-GW_{out}-\Delta S
$$

### Empirical method

$$
E=Kf(u)(e_s-e_a)
$$

### Groundwater fluctuations

$$
ET=S_y(24a+b)
$$

Quantitative skills: identify the correct method; keep all inputs on a common time and unit basis; distinguish volume, depth, and rate; preserve the sign of $b$; and show all conversions.

# Module 05 - Evaporation and Transpiration

**Central question:** How do energy, atmospheric conditions, water-body and soil properties, and vegetation determine the direction and rate of water transfer to the atmosphere by evaporation and transpiration?

## Highest-Yield Exam Points

1. **Evaporation** changes liquid water, or a solid through sublimation, into atmospheric vapour; **transpiration** releases water vapour from vegetation through stomata.
2. **Evapotranspiration (ET)** combines evaporation from free water, interception, soil, and other surfaces with transpiration from vegetation.
3. Solar energy drives ET. Maximum evaporation and transpiration generally occur during summer days; minimum rates generally occur during winter nights.
4. The vapour pressure deficit is $e_s-e_a$. Evaporation proceeds when it is positive; condensation proceeds when it is negative.
5. Warm, dry, windy, sunny conditions favour evaporation, but wind cannot raise evaporation above the maximum rate set mainly by available radiation, temperature, and humidity.
6. Fresh water evaporates faster than saline water when other conditions are equal. Shallow water warms faster than deep water, while deep water can release stored heat during cool periods or at night.
7. Average evaporation rate decreases across a larger water body as the moving air becomes more humid, although the larger body may still lose a greater total volume.
8. Soil evaporation increases with soil moisture, a shallow water table, and dark colour; vegetation cover tends to reduce it through shading, wind blockage, and increased near-surface humidity.
9. Open stomata permit transpiration. Their opening duration is controlled mainly by solar energy/day length, air temperature, and relative humidity.
10. With complete vegetative coverage, vegetation species may have little effect on regional total transpiration, while plant colour, density, leaf dimensions, growth stage, roots, and soil-water access remain important.
11. Hydrophytes access open water, mesophytes use soil moisture and may reach the water table, xerophytes use shallow dense roots in arid settings, and phreatophytes extend into the water table.
12. In Canada, evaporation is highest in spring and fall, transpiration is highest in summer and near zero in winter, and their sum makes total ET peak in summer.

## Equations and Quantitative Skills

$$
\mathrm{VPD}=e_s-e_a
$$

- If $e_s-e_a>0$, evaporation proceeds; if $e_s-e_a<0$, condensation proceeds.
- Interpret evaporation rates expressed as depth per time, such as $\mathrm{mm\,h^{-1}}$ or $\mathrm{cm\,day^{-1}}$, and accumulated evaporation as a depth in mm or cm.
- Apply the ET energy accounting pattern:

$$
E_{\mathrm{solar,ET}}=E_{\mathrm{interception}}+E_{\mathrm{transpiration}}+E_{\mathrm{soil\ evaporation}}+E_{\mathrm{net\ losses}}
$$

- Distinguish average evaporation **rate** from total evaporated **volume**.

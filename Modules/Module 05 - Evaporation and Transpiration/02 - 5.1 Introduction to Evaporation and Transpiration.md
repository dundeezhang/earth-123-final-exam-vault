# 5.1 Introduction to Evaporation and Transpiration

## Exam Focus

- Distinguish evaporation, sublimation, transpiration, and evapotranspiration.
- Identify the major sources of evaporated water and explain how stomata regulate transpiration.
- Explain why solar energy drives ET and why ET matters to water-resource and ecological decisions.
- Use $e_s-e_a$ to decide whether evaporation or condensation occurs.
- Interpret negative water balances and the visual evidence in Figures 5.1.1-5.1.6.

## Core Notes

### Evaporation

- Evaporation converts a liquid, or a solid through sublimation, to vapour or gas. It can be expressed as a rate, such as mm/hour or cm/day, or as an accumulated depth in mm or cm.
- Principal water sources are ocean surfaces; open fresh water such as lakes, rivers, and swamps; intercepted precipitation on vegetation or human structures; saturated ground or soil with abundant free water; and snow or ice through sublimation.
- Evaporation and transpiration move surface, water-body, and vegetation water into the atmosphere and provide the vapour needed for cloud formation and precipitation.

### Transpiration

- Transpiration releases liquid water held within vegetation to the atmosphere as vapour through stomata on the undersides of leaves.
- Stomata open during daylight to permit water vapour and photosynthesis-related gases to leave. They close at night, strongly limiting gaseous exchange.

![Two stomata on the underside of a leaf](../../Assets/Module%2005/stromata-on-a-camellia-leaf_5.jpg)

*Figure 5.1.1, left: photomicrograph of two stomata. Micro Discovery/Corbis Documentary/Getty Images.*

**What to notice:** The lighter guard cells surrounding each pore control whether the stoma opens or closes.

![Open and closed stoma structures](../../Assets/Module%2005/diagram-transpiration-of-plants_5.png)

*Figure 5.1.1, right: labelled stoma structure and gas exchange. blueringmedia/iStock/Getty Images.*

**What to notice:** An open stomatal pore permits water vapour and photosynthesis-related gases to pass, while closure strongly limits exchange.

- Water commonly originates below ground, enters roots, and moves through the plant. Solar radiation absorbed by leaves supports transpiration and photosynthesis.
- Vapour loss creates suction within the plant, pulling more water upward through the trunk from the soil to replace the loss.

### Evapotranspiration

- Evapotranspiration combines evaporation from free surfaces, interception, and ground with transpiration from vegetation into one value for environmental models and calculations.

![Conceptual system diagram of evapotranspiration](../../Assets/Module%2005/system-diagram-of-evapotranspiration_5.png)

*Figure 5.1.2: conceptual system diagram of evapotranspiration on Earth (Dingman, 2002, p. 2).*

**What to notice:** Solar energy reaches interception storage, snowpack, soil moisture, and streams and lakes, driving every ET pathway shown.

- ET knowledge supports climate-change analysis, water-resource planning, irrigation scheduling, freshwater and reservoir management, habitat sustainability, and analysis of groundwater-surface-water interactions.

### Water Balance Examples

- A water balance compares gains such as precipitation with losses such as evaporation.

![Lake Mead shoreline in May 2000 and May 2003](../../Assets/Module%2005/hoover-dam-and-lake-mead_5.png)

*Figure 5.1.3: Hoover Dam and Lake Mead in May 2000 (top) and May 2003 (bottom) (Allen, 2003).*

**What to notice:** The retreating shoreline connects former islands to land and exposes new islands, visual evidence of a negative water balance. The course attributes the loss to evaporation and energy-production withdrawals exceeding precipitation plus stream and groundwater inflow.

- From March 2010 through February 2012, Lake Mead lost an average of **64 million $\mathrm{m^3}$ per month** (USGS, 2021).

![Aral Sea area in 1989 and 2008](../../Assets/Module%2005/aral-sea-satellite-imagery_5.jpg)

*Figure 5.1.4: satellite imagery showing the Aral Sea in 1989 and 2008 (Producercunningham, 2014).*

**What to notice:** The fragmented, much smaller 2008 water area records a strongly negative water balance caused by extensive evaporation and overuse relative to precipitation and inflow.

### Molecular Process and Saturation

- Above absolute zero, approximately $-273\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}$, atoms and molecules are in constant motion. Their motion helps define a sample's temperature.
- Added solar or other heat energy increases molecular motion. A water molecule with enough energy can escape a liquid surface and enter the atmospheric gas phase.

![Molecular process of evaporation from heated water](../../Assets/Module%2005/evaporation-vector-illustration_5.jpg)

*Figure 5.1.5: conceptual diagram of evaporation from heated water. VectorMine/iStock/Getty Images.*

**What to notice:** Energy from an external source increases molecular motion, allowing the highest-energy molecules at the liquid surface to escape as vapour.

- Evaporation requires the air immediately above the water to be below saturation. Saturated air has $e_a=e_s$ at that temperature and 100% relative humidity.
- Condensation returns water vapour to liquid when the air is completely saturated. The net exchange depends on whether more molecules leave the surface than return to it.

![Vapour pressure and saturation curve at 10 degrees Celsius](../../Assets/Module%2005/vapour-pressure-graphs-5-1-lecture-content_5.jpg)

*Figure 5.1.6: actual and saturation vapour pressures at the same temperature. Course Author(s) and University of Waterloo.*

**What to notice:** At $10\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}$, $e_s$ is about $1.0\ \mathrm{kPa}$. Air at $e_{a1}\approx0.5\ \mathrm{kPa}$ supports evaporation, while $e_{a2}\approx2.75\ \mathrm{kPa}$ lies above saturation and supports condensation.

- Evaporation continues until the overlying air reaches saturation and outward and inward molecular rates are equal, the water disappears, or the energy source is removed.
- Still air saturates rapidly because vapour is not carried away. Warm, dry, windy conditions keep the vapour pressure deficit high and are most favourable for evaporation.

> [!important]
> Evapotranspiration includes both surface evaporation and plant transpiration. The embedded course check counts evaporation from water bodies, soil, and vegetation **and** plant water used in growth/transpiration as parts of ET.

## Equations and Units

### Vapour Pressure Deficit

$$
\mathrm{VPD}=e_s-e_a
$$

$$
e_s-e_a>0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \text{evaporation}
$$

$$
e_s-e_a<0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \text{condensation}
$$

- $e_s$: saturation vapour pressure at the stated temperature, in kPa.
- $e_a$: actual vapour pressure of the air, in kPa.
- At $e_s=e_a$, relative humidity is 100% and net molecular exchange is at equilibrium.

![Knowledge-check point below the saturation curve](../../Assets/Module%2005/vapour-pressure-graphs-5-1-concept-check_5.jpg)

*Course knowledge-check graph based on Figure 5.1.6. Course Author(s) and University of Waterloo.*

**What to notice:** The point at $10\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and $e_a=0.5\ \mathrm{kPa}$ lies below the saturation curve, giving $\mathrm{VPD}=1.0-0.5=0.5\ \mathrm{kPa}>0$, so evaporation occurs.

### Rate, Depth, and Volume Patterns

- Accumulated evaporation depth equals rate multiplied by elapsed time when the rate is assumed constant.
- The March 2010-February 2012 interval is 24 months. Applying the stated average Lake Mead loss gives $64\times24=1{,}536$ million $\mathrm{m^3}$, or $1.536$ billion $\mathrm{m^3}$, under the simplifying assumption that the average applies across the full interval.

## Common Confusions

- **Sublimation is evaporation from a solid:** ice or snow goes directly to vapour.
- **Stomata do not pull water by themselves:** vapour loss through stomata creates suction in the connected plant circulation system.
- **Saturation is temperature-specific:** compare $e_a$ with $e_s$ at the same temperature.
- **At 100% relative humidity:** the air is saturated; additional net evaporation stops and condensation can occur.
- **A negative water balance is not evaporation alone:** the Lake Mead and Aral Sea examples combine multiple inflows and outflows, although evaporation is a major loss.

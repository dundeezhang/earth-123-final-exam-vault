# Module 05 Quiz Bank

## Multiple Choice

1. Which process moves water directly from snow or ice into atmospheric vapour?
   - A. Condensation
   - B. Sublimation
   - C. Interception
   - D. Transpiration

2. Which description best defines evapotranspiration?
   - A. Evaporation from oceans only
   - B. Transpiration from vegetation only
   - C. Evaporation from free water, interception, ground, and other surfaces combined with plant transpiration
   - D. Condensation plus precipitation

3. At $10\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}$, air over water has $e_a=0.5\ \mathrm{kPa}$ while $e_s\approx1.0\ \mathrm{kPa}$. What process occurs?
   - A. Evaporation
   - B. Condensation
   - C. Sublimation only
   - D. No exchange because the air is saturated

4. What is the most important meteorological control on evaporation and transpiration?
   - A. Wind
   - B. Temperature
   - C. Solar radiation
   - D. Water depth

5. Why does increasing wind eventually stop increasing evaporation rate?
   - A. Wind always cools water to freezing.
   - B. The potential rate remains limited mainly by radiation, temperature, and relative humidity.
   - C. Wind raises salinity until evaporation stops.
   - D. Wind closes all stomata.

6. Which water body should have the highest average summer evaporation rate under otherwise comparable conditions?
   - A. A small, shallow freshwater lake
   - B. A large, shallow freshwater lake
   - C. A small, deep freshwater lake
   - D. A large, deep saline water body

7. Why does the course expect greater wind exposure across Lake Ontario than Lake Michigan under prevailing west-to-east winds?
   - A. Lake Ontario is always warmer.
   - B. Lake Ontario is fresh and Lake Michigan is saline.
   - C. Lake Ontario's orientation gives the air a longer water-surface path in the prevailing direction.
   - D. Lake Ontario has no vegetation around it.

8. Which combination explains why vegetation cover tends to reduce soil evaporation?
   - A. More shade, less wind at the soil, and higher local humidity
   - B. Less shade, faster wind, and lower local humidity
   - C. Higher salinity, darker soil, and a deeper lake
   - D. Closed roots, lower permeability, and lower albedo

9. Under complete regional vegetative coverage, which statement does the course make about vegetation type?
   - A. Species identity is the only control on transpiration.
   - B. Different crop species may transpire similar totals over time and space.
   - C. Wheat never transpires as much as hay.
   - D. Stomatal size has no relationship to transpiration.

10. Which root group extends into the local water table and can extract large quantities each day?
   - A. Hydrophytes
   - B. Mesophytes
   - C. Xerophytes
   - D. Phreatophytes

## True or False

11. Stomata generally open at night and close during daylight.

12. Evaporation cannot occur at low temperatures.

13. When all other conditions are equal, increasing salinity decreases evaporation.

14. A larger water body can have a lower average evaporation rate but a larger total evaporated volume.

15. Compared with early-season crops, mature late-season crops can increase transpiration while reducing soil evaporation.

16. In the Canadian annual pattern, evaporation and transpiration both reach their maximum in summer.

## Short Answer

17. List the five principal source categories of water for evaporation identified in section 5.1.

18. Explain what makes the Lake Mead and Aral Sea examples negative water balances.

19. State the evaporation, condensation, and equilibrium conditions in terms of $e_s-e_a$.

20. Name the four main meteorological factors controlling evaporation and give one sentence on the role of each.

21. Contrast an early-season agricultural field with a late-season field in terms of leaf area, roots, wind exposure, soil evaporation, and transpiration.

22. Name four confirmed vegetation characteristics, other than root group, that affect transpiration.

## Calculations

26. At a stated temperature, $e_s=1.00\ \mathrm{kPa}$ and $e_a=0.65\ \mathrm{kPa}$. Calculate VPD and identify the expected process.

27. At $10\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}$, use the Figure 5.1.6 values $e_s=1.0\ \mathrm{kPa}$ and $e_a=2.75\ \mathrm{kPa}$. Calculate $e_s-e_a$ and identify the expected process.

28. Lake Mead lost an average of 64 million $\mathrm{m^3}$ per month from March 2010 through February 2012. Assuming that average applies uniformly across all 24 months, calculate the cumulative loss in billion $\mathrm{m^3}$.

29. An illustrative ET energy budget has 120 energy units available. Interception evaporation uses 20 units, transpiration uses 50, and soil evaporation uses 30. Use the section 5.3 equation to calculate net losses including heat and albedo.

30. A constant evaporation rate is $4\ \mathrm{mm\,h^{-1}}$ for 3 hours. Calculate the accumulated evaporation depth.

<summary>Answer key</summary>

1. **B. Sublimation.** It is the direct transfer from solid snow or ice to vapour. Citation: [[02 - 5.1 Introduction to Evaporation and Transpiration|Course section 5.1]].

2. **C.** ET combines evaporation from free water, interception, ground, and other surfaces with vegetation transpiration. Citation: [[02 - 5.1 Introduction to Evaporation and Transpiration|Course section 5.1]].

3. **A. Evaporation.** $e_s-e_a=1.0-0.5=0.5\ \mathrm{kPa}>0$. Citation: [[02 - 5.1 Introduction to Evaporation and Transpiration|Course section 5.1]].

4. **C. Solar radiation.** The course identifies incoming solar energy as the most important meteorological factor. Citation: [[03 - 5.2 Meteorological, Geographical, and Soil Factors Affecting Evaporation|Course section 5.2]].

5. **B.** Wind removes moist air, but radiation, temperature, and humidity still set the maximum or potential rate. Citation: [[03 - 5.2 Meteorological, Geographical, and Soil Factors Affecting Evaporation|Course section 5.2]].

6. **A. A small, shallow freshwater lake.** Small size limits the distance over which the air becomes more humid, shallow water warms rapidly, and fresh water evaporates faster than saline water when other conditions match. Citation: [[03 - 5.2 Meteorological, Geographical, and Soil Factors Affecting Evaporation|Course section 5.2]].

7. **C.** Lake Ontario's orientation provides a longer west-to-east path of contact between air and water. Citation: [[03 - 5.2 Meteorological, Geographical, and Soil Factors Affecting Evaporation|Course section 5.2]].

8. **A.** Vegetation shades soil, blocks wind, and raises local relative humidity through transpiration. Citation: [[03 - 5.2 Meteorological, Geographical, and Soil Factors Affecting Evaporation|Course section 5.2]].

9. **B.** With complete coverage, wheat, barley, or hay may transpire similar totals over time and space, though this remains an active research area. Citation: [[04 - 5.3 Vegetational, Geographical, and Soil Factors Affecting Transpiration|Course section 5.3]].

10. **D. Phreatophytes.** Their roots reach the local water table and extract large daily quantities. Citation: [[04 - 5.3 Vegetational, Geographical, and Soil Factors Affecting Transpiration|Course section 5.3]].

11. **False.** Stomata open during daylight and close at night. Citation: [[02 - 5.1 Introduction to Evaporation and Transpiration|Course section 5.1]].

12. **False.** Low-temperature evaporation can occur if the air is below saturation, though the rate may be slow. Citation: [[03 - 5.2 Meteorological, Geographical, and Soil Factors Affecting Evaporation|Course section 5.2]].

13. **True.** Evaporation decreases as salinity increases when other conditions are equal. Citation: [[03 - 5.2 Meteorological, Geographical, and Soil Factors Affecting Evaporation|Course section 5.2]].

14. **True.** Average rate falls as air becomes more humid over a longer water-surface path, but a larger total area can still yield a larger volume. Citation: [[03 - 5.2 Meteorological, Geographical, and Soil Factors Affecting Evaporation|Course section 5.2]].

15. **True.** Mature crops add leaf area and transpiration while shading and sheltering the soil. Citation: [[04 - 5.3 Vegetational, Geographical, and Soil Factors Affecting Transpiration|Course section 5.3]].

16. **False.** Canadian evaporation peaks in spring and fall; transpiration and total ET peak in summer. Citation: [[04 - 5.3 Vegetational, Geographical, and Soil Factors Affecting Transpiration|Course section 5.3]].

17. **Ocean surfaces; open fresh water; intercepted precipitation on vegetation or structures; saturated ground/soil or other freely wetted surfaces; and snow/ice through sublimation.** Citation: [[02 - 5.1 Introduction to Evaporation and Transpiration|Course section 5.1]].

18. **Outflows exceed inflows.** For Lake Mead, evaporation and water losses associated with energy production exceeded precipitation plus stream and groundwater inflow. For the Aral Sea, extensive evaporation and overuse exceeded precipitation and inflow. Citation: [[02 - 5.1 Introduction to Evaporation and Transpiration|Course section 5.1]].

19. **Evaporation:** $e_s-e_a>0$. **Condensation:** $e_s-e_a<0$. **Saturation/equilibrium:** $e_s-e_a=0$. Citation: [[02 - 5.1 Introduction to Evaporation and Transpiration|Course section 5.1]].

20. **Solar radiation** supplies the controlling energy; **temperature** sets saturation vapour pressure; **humidity** determines the available moisture deficit; **wind** removes moist surface air and maintains the gradient up to the potential-rate limit. Citation: [[03 - 5.2 Meteorological, Geographical, and Soil Factors Affecting Evaporation|Course section 5.2]].

21. **Early season:** small leaf area, shallow roots above the water table, little wind shelter, high exposed-soil evaporation, and low transpiration. **Late season:** large leaf area, deeper roots reaching the water table, more wind shelter and local humidity, lower soil evaporation, and higher transpiration. Citation: [[04 - 5.3 Vegetational, Geographical, and Soil Factors Affecting Transpiration|Course section 5.3]].

22. **Plant colour, vegetation density, leaf dimensions, and stage of growth.** Citation: [[04 - 5.3 Vegetational, Geographical, and Soil Factors Affecting Transpiration|Course section 5.3]].

23. **A-3, B-4, C-1, D-2.** Hydrophytes reach water directly; mesophytes use soil moisture; xerophytes have shallow dense roots; phreatophytes reach the water table. Citation: [[04 - 5.3 Vegetational, Geographical, and Soil Factors Affecting Transpiration|Course section 5.3]].

24. **A-2, B-1, C-4, D-3.** These pair water depth, soil colour, salinity, and water-table position with their course effects. Citation: [[03 - 5.2 Meteorological, Geographical, and Soil Factors Affecting Evaporation|Course section 5.2]].

25. **A-2, B-4, C-1, D-3.** Citation: Figure 5.1.1 in [[02 - 5.1 Introduction to Evaporation and Transpiration|Course section 5.1]], Figure 5.2.1 in [[03 - 5.2 Meteorological, Geographical, and Soil Factors Affecting Evaporation|Course section 5.2]], and Figures 5.3.2 and 5.3.4 in [[04 - 5.3 Vegetational, Geographical, and Soil Factors Affecting Transpiration|Course section 5.3]].

27. $e_s-e_a=1.0-2.75=-1.75\ \mathrm{kPa}$. The negative value indicates **condensation**. Citation: [[02 - 5.1 Introduction to Evaporation and Transpiration|Course section 5.1]].

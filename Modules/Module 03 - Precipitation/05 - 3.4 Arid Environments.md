# 3.4 Arid Environments

Up: [[00 - EARTH 123 Final Exam Home]]

Module: [[00 - Module 03 Overview]] | Definitions: [[01 - Main Definitions]]

## Exam Focus

- Connect descending circulation near $30^\circ$ N/S to global desert distribution.
- State the course's annual-precipitation definition of an arid region.
- Explain why deserts can be hot or cold and why running water still shapes them.
- Read the dry/humid climate-boundary table using both temperature and rainfall seasonality.

## Core Notes

### Drought and water balance

Hydrologists study precipitation and its absence. Droughts and sequences of dry days reduce a region's water balance, requiring households and farmers to conserve water. Urban restrictions may limit lawn and garden watering or pool filling.

### Global circulation and desert distribution

Large-scale circulation controls where precipitation occurs and where it does not. Warm, moist air rises near the equator; much drier air descends from aloft near $30^\circ$ N and S. This descending air favours broad dry regions on every continent.

![Global distribution of arid deserts and semi-arid steppes](../../Assets/Module%2003/3.4-arid-semi-arid-lands.jpg)
*Figure 3.4.1. Global distribution of arid and semi-arid lands. Tarbuck and Lutgens (2002, p. 366).*

**What to notice:** Many major deserts and steppes cluster near the Tropics of Cancer and Capricorn, close to the descending limbs of circulation around $30^\circ$ N/S, though mountains and continental position modify the pattern.

### What defines an arid environment?

The course defines an arid or desert region as receiving **no more than $25\ \mathrm{cm}$ of precipitation in an entire year**, whether rain, snow, or a combination.

> [!important] Keep the thresholds separate
> The $25\ \mathrm{cm/year}$ value is the course's general arid-region definition. Table 3.4.1 instead gives temperature- and rainfall-season-dependent boundaries between dry and humid climates.

Deserts are not necessarily hot:

- subtropical deserts occur where warm, dry air descends;
- polar deserts occur because cold air cannot hold as much moisture as warm air, greatly limiting precipitation.

Despite little annual precipitation, running water is one of the most active forces shaping deserts because rare precipitation can generate flash floods.

### Temperature, rainfall season, and the dry/humid boundary

**Table 3.4.1: Average Annual Precipitation Defining the Boundary between Dry and Humid Climates**

| Average annual temperature ($^\circ\mathrm{C}$) | Winter rainfall maximum (cm) | Even distribution (cm) | Summer rainfall maximum (cm) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | 10 | 24 | 38 |
| 10 | 20 | 34 | 48 |
| 15 | 30 | 44 | 58 |
| 20 | 40 | 54 | 68 |
| 25 | 50 | 64 | 78 |
| 30 | 60 | 74 | 88 |

The boundary rises by $10\ \mathrm{cm}$ for each $5^\circ\mathrm{C}$ increase in average annual temperature within each rainfall-season column. At a given temperature, the summer-maximum boundary is highest, the winter-maximum boundary is lowest, and even distribution lies between them.

> [!example] Table-reading pattern
> At an average annual temperature of $20^\circ\mathrm{C}$, select the column that matches rainfall seasonality: $40\ \mathrm{cm}$ for a winter maximum, $54\ \mathrm{cm}$ for even distribution, or $68\ \mathrm{cm}$ for a summer maximum.

### Global precipitation pattern

![Global map of annual total precipitation in centimetres](../../Assets/Module%2003/3.4-global-annual-precipitation.jpg)
*Figure 3.4.2. Global annual total precipitation. Nelson Institute Center for Sustainability and the Global Environment (n.d.).*

**What to notice:** Darker purple marks wetter regions, including the equatorial belt, while pale subtropical interiors align with many deserts near $30^\circ$ N/S. Equatorial totals reach about **$250\ \mathrm{cm/year}$**, compared with roughly **$70-90\ \mathrm{cm/year}$** in Waterloo Region.

## Common Confusions

- **Desert does not mean hot.** The defining feature is low precipitation; polar regions can be deserts.
- **The general $25\ \mathrm{cm}$ arid definition and Table 3.4.1 answer different questions.** Do not substitute one for the other.
- **Low annual rainfall does not make running water unimportant.** Rare flash floods can make it a dominant landscape-shaping force.
- **Equatorial warmth does not imply dryness.** It drives strong evaporation, but rising moist air then cools, condenses, and produces abundant precipitation.

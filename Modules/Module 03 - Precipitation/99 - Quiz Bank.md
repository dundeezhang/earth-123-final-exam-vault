## Multiple Choice

1. Which item counts as precipitation under the course's broad definition?
   - A. Only rain that falls from a cloud
   - B. Dew transferred from the atmosphere to grass
   - C. River discharge to the ocean
   - D. Groundwater moving through an aquifer

2. What is the key hydrologic distinction between liquid- and solid-phase precipitation?
   - A. Only solid precipitation contains freshwater.
   - B. Liquid precipitation is always regional; solid precipitation is always local.
   - C. Liquid precipitation immediately becomes active in the hydrologic cycle, while solid precipitation tends to remain stored until melting.
   - D. Solid precipitation forms without atmospheric moisture.

3. Which event best matches convective lifting?
   - A. Long, gentle rain over a broad region ahead of a warm front
   - B. A local, short-duration summer thunderstorm with intense rain and hail
   - C. Dry leeward conditions behind a mountain range
   - D. Persistent polar descent

4. Based on the Canadian relief knowledge check, where is orographic precipitation most likely among the listed regions?
   - A. Ontario
   - B. Saskatchewan
   - C. Manitoba
   - D. British Columbia

5. In the course's national annual-precipitation knowledge check, which control is identified as primary?
   - A. Latitude
   - B. Ocean currents
   - C. Random variation
   - D. Regional factors

6. Why are broad arid zones common near $30^\circ$ N and S?
   - A. Cold polar air rises there.
   - B. Warm ocean water freezes there.
   - C. Dry air descends and warms there, favouring evaporation and suppressing precipitation.
   - D. Mountain ranges occur continuously along both latitudes.

7. Approximately what share of global evaporation originates from oceans?
   - A. 10%
   - B. 30%
   - C. 60%
   - D. 90%

8. Which condition marks saturation?
   - A. $e=0$
   - B. $e_s=0$
   - C. $e=e_s$ and $R_h=1.0$
   - D. $e>e_s$ and $R_h=0$

9. What density ratio is typical of new fresh snowfall in the course material?
   - A. 0.006 or 0.6%
   - B. 0.06 or 6%
   - C. 0.6 or 60%
   - D. 1.0 or 100%

10. Under the course's general definition, an arid region receives no more than:
   - A. $2.5\ \mathrm{cm}$ of precipitation per year

   - C. $70\ \mathrm{cm}$ of precipitation per year
   - D. $250\ \mathrm{cm}$ of precipitation per year

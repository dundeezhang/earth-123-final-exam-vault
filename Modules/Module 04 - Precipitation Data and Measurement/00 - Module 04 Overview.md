# Module 04: Precipitation Data and Measurement

## Highest-Yield Exam Points

1. Precipitation data may be expressed as depth, area, volume, or rate; units must be compatible before a calculation.
2. Gauge-network design depends on study-area size and purpose, precipitation and gauge type, topography, desired accuracy, and gauge number and distribution.
3. Short-duration, small-area events are generally more spatially variable than long-duration, large-area events, so they demand denser sampling.
4. Non-recording gauges require a person to retrieve observations and do not measure rainfall intensity; recording gauges include weighing, float, and tipping-bucket designs.
5. A tipping-bucket gauge converts a known volume per tip into depth or intensity, but precipitation below the volume needed for a tip is not recorded.
6. Important errors include mechanical failure, observer error, wind under-catch, poor placement or orientation, and evaporation from the collector.
7. A gauge should be in a flat, open, low-wind site and at least four times the height of the nearest object away from that object.
8. Snow gauges are elevated to avoid burial, commonly use wind shields, and measure melted snow as water equivalent; snow density can be found from a snow core.
9. Missing point data can be interpolated using nearby recorded totals normalized by each station's long-term normal precipitation.
10. Station moves or environmental and equipment changes can create artificial trends; a slope-based adjustment factor can place old and new data on a common basis.
11. Areal precipitation can be estimated by arithmetic average, the isohyetal method, or the area-weighted Thiessen Polygon Method.
12. Arithmetic averaging is fast and most suitable for relatively uniform precipitation over flat terrain; topographic or spatial variability increases its error.

## Equations and Quantitative Skills

- Rainfall intensity: $I = P/\Delta t$.
- Snow-core volume and density: $V = A d$ and $\rho = m/V$.
- Missing-station interpolation: $P_x = \dfrac{N_x}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{P_i}{N_i}$.
- Long-term adjustment factor: $K = \dfrac{\text{slope after change}}{\text{slope before change}}$.
- Arithmetic average: $\bar P = \dfrac{1}{G}\sum_{g=1}^{G}p_g$.
- Isohyetal volume: $V = A\bar P$, after converting depth to the same length unit used by the area.
- Thiessen estimate: $\bar P = \sum_i w_iP_i$, where the polygon area fractions satisfy $\sum_i w_i = 1$. The result retains the precipitation-depth unit.

> [!tip]
> For any volume problem, convert precipitation depth first: centimetres paired with square metres must be converted to metres before multiplication.

# 1.2 The Scientific Method

## Exam Focus

Know how a testable explanation connects observations, predictions, measurements, analysis, and conclusions. Distinguish hypotheses from predictions and independent variables from dependent variables.

## Study Framework

### Investigation sequence

1. **Observe:** identify a pattern or event.
2. **Question:** define a focused problem that evidence can address.
3. **Hypothesize:** propose a testable explanation.
4. **Predict:** state what should occur if the hypothesis is supported.
5. **Test:** collect observations or measurements under suitable comparisons or controls.
6. **Analyze:** compare the evidence with the prediction.
7. **Conclude:** support, revise, or reject the hypothesis.
8. **Repeat and communicate:** check whether the result is reproducible and open to evaluation.

### Hydrology application

**Observation:** A stream responds more rapidly to storms after nearby development.

**Question:** Does increased impervious surface area increase storm runoff?

**Hypothesis:** Pavement reduces infiltration and sends more water rapidly to the stream.

**Prediction:** More-developed watersheds will show shorter lag times and higher peak discharge during comparable storms.

**Test and data:** Compare rainfall, land cover, and discharge records across watersheds or through time.

**Conclusion:** Determine whether the observations match the prediction closely enough to support the hypothesis, while identifying uncertainty and alternative explanations.

### Variables and controls

- **Independent variable:** the proposed influence, such as impervious surface percentage.
- **Dependent variable:** the measured response, such as peak discharge.
- **Controlled variables:** conditions kept comparable, such as rainfall depth, storm duration, or watershed area.
- Field hydrology cannot always isolate every condition. Scientists therefore document uncertainty and use repeated observations, comparisons, and statistical analysis.

### Interpreting evidence

- Evidence can support a hypothesis without proving it permanently.
- A result that conflicts with a prediction can reveal a weak hypothesis, poor measurement, uncontrolled conditions, or an incomplete explanation.
- Repetition and independent evaluation increase confidence.
- A scientific explanation should change when stronger evidence requires revision.

## Common Confusions

- **Question vs. hypothesis:** a question identifies what is being investigated; a hypothesis proposes an explanation.
- **Hypothesis vs. prediction:** the hypothesis explains; the prediction states the expected result.
- **Correlation vs. causation:** two variables changing together does not establish that one caused the other.
- **Supporting vs. proving:** evidence can increase confidence, but scientific conclusions remain open to revision.
- **Precision vs. accuracy:** repeated fine-scale values may be precise while still differing from the true value.

## Highest-Yield Exam Points

- Groundwater is water beneath the land surface that fully saturates pores; the water table marks the top of the saturated zone.
- Aquifers store and transmit water. The four course types are unconfined, confined, semi-confined (leaky), and karstic.
- Aquitards and confining layers have very low permeability and restrict groundwater flow.
- Glacially deposited sand and gravel form important southern Ontario aquifers; moraines store recharge and feed springs, streams, and rivers.
- Groundwater moves from high to low hydraulic head. In homogeneous geology, flow lines cross equipotential contours at right angles.
- Piezometer networks reveal water-table elevation, hydraulic gradient, and whether groundwater levels are stable, rising, or falling.
- High porosity does not guarantee high permeability: clay has many poorly connected pores, while gravel has large, connected pores.
- Hydraulic head is elevation head plus pressure head; confined water may rise to a potentiometric surface and may produce an artesian well.
- Darcy's Law relates flow to hydraulic conductivity, cross-sectional area, and hydraulic gradient; dividing Darcy flux by porosity gives groundwater velocity.
- Streams may gain groundwater, lose water to the subsurface, lose water above a perched table, or carry flow through from one bank to the other.
- Contaminants can enter groundwater through runoff, agriculture, septic systems, road salt, spills, pesticides, pharmaceuticals, and saltwater incursion.
- At Walkerton, runoff carrying manure entered open Well #5, and fractured karst geology connected the surface aquifer to deeper semi-confined drinking-water aquifers.

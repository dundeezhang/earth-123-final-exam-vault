## Core Notes

### Head and Pressure

- Water-table elevation is ground-surface elevation minus depth to water.
- Well-bottom elevation is ground-surface elevation minus well depth.
- Hydrostatic pressure head is hydraulic head minus well-bottom elevation.
- Water pressure increases with pressure head according to $p=\rho g h_p$.
- Water moves from higher hydraulic head toward lower hydraulic head.

### Hydraulic Gradient

Hydraulic gradient is change in hydraulic head divided by distance:

$$
i=\frac{\Delta h}{L}
$$

It is dimensionless when head and distance use the same length unit. Compare heads first to state flow direction; use the gradient magnitude in the Assignment 5 calculations.

### Hydraulic Conductivity and Darcy Specific Discharge

- Hydraulic conductivity $K$ measures how readily a material transmits water and has velocity units such as $\mathrm{m/s}$ or $\mathrm{m/day}$.
- Hydraulic conductivity varies by many orders of magnitude. Clay and unfractured igneous/metamorphic rock are low-$K$ materials; sand, gravel, fractured rock, and limestone with solution openings are higher-$K$ materials.

![Log-scale ranges of hydraulic conductivity for rocks and unconsolidated materials](../../Assets/Module%2009/9-4-hydraulic-conductivity-range.png)

*Retained course figure: hydraulic-conductivity ranges for geologic materials. The original caption and attribution are unrecoverable because the saved 9.4 HTML is empty.*

**What to notice:** Read the horizontal axis logarithmically: a small visual shift can represent orders of magnitude in conductivity. Fractures and solution openings can make rock much more transmissive than its intact form.

![Layered sand and gravel with contrasting hydraulic conductivities](../../Assets/Module%2009/9-4-heterogeneous-soil-conductivity.png)

*Retained course figure: heterogeneous sand and gravel conductivity. The original caption and attribution are unrecoverable because the saved 9.4 HTML is empty.*

**What to notice:** The gravel layer has the larger labelled $K$ and therefore transmits water more readily than the surrounding sand. In layered ground, the fastest pathway can follow the more conductive unit.

Darcy specific discharge:

$$
q=Ki
$$

If total cross-sectional area is required, $Q=qA$.

### Pore-Water Velocity and Travel Time

Specific discharge treats the full bulk area as available for flow. Actual pore-water velocity accounts for the void fraction:

$$
v=\frac{q}{n}
$$

Travel time over path length $L$ is:

$$
t=\frac{L}{|v|}
$$

Convert porosity from percent to a decimal before division.

### Course Worked Values: Wells A, B, and C

The Assignment 5 notes use a ground elevation of **425 m**, a well-bottom depth of **41 m**, and depths to water of **27 m**, **28 m**, and **30 m** for Wells A, B, and C.

1. Well-bottom elevation: $425-41=384\ \text{m}$.
2. Hydraulic heads/water-table elevations: A $=425-27=398\ \text{m}$; B $=397\ \text{m}$; C $=395\ \text{m}$.
3. Pressure heads above the well bottom: A $=14\ \text{m}$; B $=13\ \text{m}$; C $=11\ \text{m}$.
4. With $\rho=1000\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ and $g=9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$:

| Well | Bottom elevation (m) | Hydraulic head (m) | Pressure head (m) | Pressure (kPa) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 384 | 398 | 14 | 137.34 |
| B | 384 | 397 | 13 | 127.53 |
| C | 384 | 395 | 11 | 107.91 |

### Course Worked Values: Gradient, Discharge, Velocity, and Travel

| Interval | Gradient calculation | $i$ | Flow direction |
|---|---|---:|---|
| A to B | $(398-397)/50$ | 0.020 | A to B |
| B to C | $(397-395)/50$ | 0.040 | B to C |
| A to C | $(398-395)/100$ | 0.030 | A to C |

The notes convert $K=2.5\times10^{-5}\ \text{m/s}$ to **$2.16\ \text{m/day}$**.

| Interval | Specific-discharge calculation | $q$ |
|---|---|---:|
| A to B | $2.16(0.020)$ | $0.0432\ \text{m/day}$ |
| A to C | $2.16(0.030)$ | $0.0648\ \text{m/day}$ |

For A to C, with porosity $n=0.25$:

$$
v=\frac{0.0648}{0.25}=0.2592\ \text{m/day}=25.92\ \text{cm/day}
$$

For a $100\ \text{m}$ path:

$$
t=\frac{100}{0.2592}=385.8\ \text{days}
$$

## Equations and Units

| Quantity | Equation | Typical units in local course notes |
|---|---|---|
| Water-table elevation | $z_{\mathrm{wt}}=z_{\mathrm{ground}}-d_{\mathrm{water}}$ | m |
| Well-bottom elevation | $z_b=z_{\mathrm{ground}}-d_{\mathrm{well}}$ | m |
| Pressure head | $h_p=h-z_b$ | m of water |
| Pressure | $p=\rho g h_p$ | Pa; divide by $1{,}000$ for kPa |
| Hydraulic gradient | $i=\Delta h/L$ | dimensionless |
| Specific discharge | $q=Ki$ | m/s or m/day |
| Pore-water velocity | $v=q/n$ | m/s or m/day |
| Travel time | $t=L/|v|$ | s or days, matching velocity units |

## Common Confusions

- Depth below ground and elevation above a datum are opposite directions; subtract depth from ground elevation.
- Hydraulic head is an elevation, while pressure head is the water-column height above the measurement point.
- Convert $K$ into the desired time unit before using it; the course notes convert $2.5\times10^{-5}\ \text{m/s}$ to $2.16\ \text{m/day}$.
- Use porosity as a decimal: $25\%=0.25$.
- Specific discharge and pore velocity are not equal; $v=q/n$, so pore velocity is larger when $0<n<1$.
- Gradient magnitude gives steepness; the higher-head well determines flow direction.

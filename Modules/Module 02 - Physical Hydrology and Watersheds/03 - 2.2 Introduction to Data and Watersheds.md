# 2.2 Introduction to Data and Watersheds

Up: [[00 - EARTH 123 Final Exam Home|Home]]

Module: [[Modules/Module 02 - Physical Hydrology and Watersheds/00 - Module 02 Overview|Overview]] | Definitions: [[Modules/Module 02 - Physical Hydrology and Watersheds/01 - Main Definitions|Definitions]]

## Exam Focus

- Distinguish length-per-time data from volume-per-time data.
- Apply the conservation equation $\Delta S=I-O$.
- Interpret precipitation and discharge on a hydrograph.
- Define a watershed using its topographic divide, tributaries, and outlet.
- Recognize nested subwatersheds and continental divides.

## Core Notes

### Hydrologic data and units

Hydrologic variables change through time, so both individual observations and continuous records matter. Sampling intervals may range from seconds to years. Scientists use long records to identify trends, anomalous values, storms, floods, droughts, and seasonal patterns.

Two common data types are:

| Data type | Dimensions | Typical units | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Depth or length rate | $L/T$ | $\mathrm{mm/h}$, $\mathrm{m/day}$ | Rainfall, evaporation, infiltration |
| Volumetric flow rate | $V/T$; dimensions $L^3/T$ | $\mathrm{m^3/s}$, $\mathrm{m^3/day}$ | River discharge, groundwater flow |

Converting depth to volume requires an area:

$$
V=dA
$$

Convert all dimensions and time intervals to compatible units before combining terms.

**What to notice:** The diagram contains water depths, areas, volumes, temperatures, velocities, and residence times. A valid calculation must preserve the dimensions of each variable.

### Systems and conservation

A **system** has defined boundaries, inputs, outputs, and internal storage. Simplification reduces detail, but a well-chosen model can retain the processes required for a question.

![Basic system with input, storage, and output](../../Assets/Module%2002/2.2-basic-system.png)
*Figure 2.2.3: Basic hydrologic system. Course Author(s) and University of Waterloo.*

**What to notice:** The system boundary determines what counts as input or output. Internal water is storage, and its change follows conservation.

$$
\Delta S=I-O
$$

- Positive result: storage increased.
- Negative result: storage decreased.
- Zero result: steady state.

### Hydrographs

A **hydrograph** plots discharge through time and may include precipitation. In the source example, precipitation occurs on Day 1, river discharge rises to a peak on Day 2, then declines.

![Conceptual storm hydrograph with precipitation and discharge](../../Assets/Module%2002/2.2-hydrograph.png)
*Figure 2.2.4: Conceptual hydrograph showing rainfall, baseflow, storm flow, and flood volume. Course Author(s) and University of Waterloo.*

**What to notice:** The discharge response lags behind rainfall because water requires time to reach the channel and outlet. The highest part of the curve represents peak flow and possible floodwater.

### Watersheds and drainage boundaries

A **watershed** is the total area drained by a river and its tributaries toward a defined outlet. The outer boundary follows raised topography called a divide.

![Mississippi watershed draining toward the Gulf of Mexico](../../Assets/Module%2002/2.2-mississippi-watershed.jpg)
*Figure 2.2.5: Mississippi watershed, the largest watershed in North America. NASA Scientific Visualization Studio (2016).*

**What to notice:** Water falling inside the highlighted boundary ultimately drains south to the Gulf of Mexico. The Missouri and Ohio systems form nested subwatersheds.

A **continental divide** separates drainage toward opposite sides of a continent. Across western North America, water on one side drains toward the Pacific, while water on the other side may enter the Mississippi system and reach the Gulf of Mexico.

![Conceptual watershed with inputs, outputs, and storage](../../Assets/Module%2002/2.2-conceptual-watershed.jpg)
*Figure 2.2.6: Conceptual watershed system. Dingman (2002, p. 12).*

**What to notice:** Raised edges form the divide. The central stream leads to the low outlet, while precipitation, groundwater, evapotranspiration, discharge, and storage cross or occupy the system.

![Real-world watershed with streams and drainage divides](../../Assets/Module%2002/2.2-real-world-watershed.png)
*Figure 2.2.7: Possible real-world watershed. Course Author(s) and University of Waterloo; VectorMine/iStock/Getty Images; tarras79/iStock/Getty Images.*

**What to notice:** Dotted lines follow high ground between channels. Water moves downhill to the nearest stream and then toward the watershed outlet.

## Common Confusions

- **A watershed is defined by an outflow point.** It is not defined by one inflow point or by precipitation alone.
- **Large area does not guarantee the largest discharge.** Climate, storage, and losses also affect flow.
- **A hydrograph is not limited to floods.** It can display ordinary river flow through time.
- **Precipitation and discharge use different units.** Rainfall commonly uses depth; discharge uses volume per time.
- **Simplification is acceptable when it preserves the required processes.** Extra detail is useful only when it materially affects the result.

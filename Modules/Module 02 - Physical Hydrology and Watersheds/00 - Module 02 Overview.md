## Highest-Yield Exam Points

1. **Physical hydrology** studies the occurrence, movement, and physical properties of water on Earth, within Earth, and in the atmosphere, in solid, liquid, and gaseous forms.
2. The four major water-resource categories are atmospheric water, surface water, groundwater, and the cryosphere.
3. Oceans contain about $97\%$ of Earth's water. Freshwater is under $3\%$, and most freshwater is frozen or underground.
4. Moving water transports sediment and contaminants. A contamination source becomes a wider hydrologic problem when water creates a pathway away from it.
5. Hydrologic data commonly use length per time, such as $\mathrm{mm/day}$, or volume per time, such as $\mathrm{m^3/s}$. Conversions must account for area and time.
6. A **system** has defined physical boundaries, inputs, outputs, and storage. Conservation gives $\Delta S=I-O$.
7. A **hydrograph** plots discharge through time and may also show precipitation. It reveals the timing and magnitude of a watershed's response.
8. A **watershed** is the area bounded by topographic divides whose water drains toward a defined outlet. Smaller subwatersheds can be nested within it.
9. The general water balance is $\Delta S=(P+G_{in}+Q_{in})-(ET+G_{out}+Q_{out})$.
10. **Steady state** means $\Delta S=0$. Inputs equal outputs, even when both sides are nonzero.
11. Hydrologic questions occur at different scales. Climate and circulation are global; the Great Lakes are regional; the Grand River and a rain barrel are watershed or local examples.
12. The Great Lakes contain over $20{,}000\ \mathrm{km^3}$ of water, and less than $1\%$ of their volume drains through the St. Lawrence River each year.
13. Great Lakes water delays shoreline frost and supplies moisture for lake-effect snow, especially east and southeast of the lakes under prevailing west-to-east winds.
14. The Grand River watershed covers about $7{,}000\ \mathrm{km^2}$, is about $300\ \mathrm{km}$ long, and drains south to Lake Erie.
15. Waterloo Region relies heavily on groundwater. The saved course page reports more than 120 wells supplying about 40 million gallons per day.

## Equations and Quantitative Skills

### Conservation equation

$$
\Delta S=I-O
$$

- $\Delta S>0$: storage increases.
- $\Delta S<0$: storage decreases.
- $\Delta S=0$: steady state.

### Watershed water balance

$$
\Delta S=(P+G_{in}+Q_{in})-(ET+G_{out}+Q_{out})
$$

Include each term only when it crosses the chosen system boundary during the study period.

### Depth-to-volume conversion

$$
V=dA
$$

Convert depth and area to compatible units first. For example, $10\ \mathrm{mm}=0.010\ \mathrm{m}$ and $1\ \mathrm{km^2}=10^6\ \mathrm{m^2}$.

### Steady state

$$
\Delta S=0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad I=O
$$

Zero change in storage does not require zero flow.

# 8.2 Soil Erosion, Degradation, and Weathering Processes

Up: [[00 - EARTH 123 Final Exam Home]]  
Module: [[00 - Module 08 Overview]]

## Exam Focus

> [!important]
> Distinguish degradation, erosion, and weathering; compare physical and chemical weathering; identify the major erosional agents and the effect of velocity; and know the field practices that reduce wind and water erosion.

## Core Notes

### Soil Degradation

- **Soil degradation** concerns the loss or declining quality of valuable topsoil near the surface, the material most important to humans, ecosystems, and agriculture.
- Course examples include desertification, erosion, topsoil deterioration, pollution, fertilizer overuse, and intensification of agriculture. Multiple processes can combine and accelerate soil loss or quality decline.
- **Figure 8.2.1:** the global map shows that many of the most populated regions also have the most degraded soils. In the embedded knowledge check, **Southeast Asia** is the correct choice for the greatest soil loss/degradation among the four listed regions.
- Degraded soil carries both environmental and economic costs. The course describes losses of billions of dollars per year through reduced soil productivity and reduced ability to grow profitable food and textile crops.
- Soil formation occurs over very long periods, while erosion can be rapid enough to observe as wind-blown sediment or muddy storm runoff.

### Soil Erosion

- **Erosion** is the physical removal and transport of soil, sediment, or other weathered material from where it formed to a new location.
- Water, wind, ice, and gravity can all erode exposed material. Greater movement velocity increases erosional effectiveness or impact.
- Water and wind can erode rapidly. Glacial ice can erode slowly but powerfully over long periods in frozen climates.
- Sediment can move hundreds of kilometres. The Mississippi example in [[02 - 8.1 Introduction to Soils and Phase Relationships#Why Soil Understanding Matters|Figure 8.1.3]] includes topsoil transported from the northern reaches of a watershed that extends into Canada.
- The Death Valley video shows that a thin layer of surface ice can move large boulders across the desert.

#### Limiting Topsoil Erosion

| Practice | Why it works |
|---|---|
| Plant windbreaks | Tree lines or fences placed perpendicular to the prevailing wind slow erosive wind. |
| Reduce field slope | Slower surface runoff has less ability to transport sediment. |
| Plough parallel to topography | Contoured fields interrupt downslope runoff where the slope cannot be reduced. |
| Build stepped terraces | Complete slope modification reduces long, fast runoff paths. |

#### Fertilizers and Soil Quality

- Protecting soil from movement is not enough if its health and quality decline.
- **Chemical fertilizers** are mined or synthetically manufactured nutrient supplements.
- **Organic fertilizers** are commonly made from animal waste or composted plant material.
- Correct quantities can be beneficial, but overapplication of either type can harm soils, plants, and animals and alter local water and soil chemistry for years.
- **Figure 8.2.2:** fertilizer overuse affects soil, groundwater, surface water, and the atmosphere. The course points ahead to organic fertilizer contamination in the Walkerton groundwater case.

### Soil Weathering

- **Weathering** includes physical, chemical, and biological processes that break rocks down to form soil. It often begins when soil, rock, cooled lava, volcanic ash, dunes, or similar mineral material is exposed to the atmosphere.
- **Physical or mechanical weathering** breaks material into smaller pieces. Each piece retains the original parent's characteristics except size.
- Physical mechanisms named by the course include gravity, freezing water, and biological activity.
- Water expands rather than contracts when it freezes. The outward force can crack and break rock.
- Tree-root growth is biological activity that physically heaves, cracks, and breaks pavement and foundations.
- **Chemical weathering** decomposes internal mineral composition and converts some or all parent material into new and different minerals.
- Water enables chemical reactions and ion transfer. The course states that little to no chemical reaction can occur in a rock or soil mass without water.

## Common Confusions

- **Degradation vs. erosion:** degradation is the broader decline or loss of topsoil quality; erosion is one degradation process involving removal and transport.
- **Weathering vs. erosion:** weathering breaks material down; erosion carries it away.
- **Physical vs. chemical weathering:** physical weathering preserves mineral composition while reducing size; chemical weathering produces different minerals.
- **Biological activity:** tree roots are biological agents, but the cracking they cause is classified here as physical weathering.
- **Organic vs. harmless:** organic fertilizer describes its source, not its impact. Overapplication of either organic or chemical fertilizer can be harmful.
- **Erosion control vs. fertilization:** contouring, terracing, and windbreaks limit physical loss; fertilizer type alone does not prevent erosion.

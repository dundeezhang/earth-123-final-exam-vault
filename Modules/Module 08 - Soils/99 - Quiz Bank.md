9. **C.** Mechanical weathering preserves composition while reducing size; chemical weathering converts material into new minerals. (Course section: [[03 - 8.2 Soil Erosion, Degradation, and Weathering Processes#Soil Weathering|8.2 Soil Weathering]])

24. **Parent material, time, climate, plants and animals, and topography.** Examples: more time allows deeper, finer, more developed soil; steep topography increases transport and leaves shallower profiles; precipitation enables chemical weathering; organisms break down material and add organic matter. 25. **With depth, organic matter and weathering generally decrease while grain size increases.** O is organic-rich; A mixes minerals and organics and contains roots; E is the lighter leached transition; B is the first subsoil with clay and silt accumulation; C contains newly weathered soil and large rock fragments; R is bedrock and not soil. (Course section: [[02 - 8.1 Introduction to Soils and Phase Relationships#The Soil Profile|8.1 The Soil Profile]])

26. Any three of: slope enabling gravity-driven flow; channelized water with greater depth and velocity; finer grains at path edges; exposed gravel and cobbles in the centre; evidence of sediment transport; human-caused vegetation loss and compaction. (Course section: [[02 - 8.1 Introduction to Soils and Phase Relationships#Erosion Evidence on Campus|8.1 Check Your Knowledge, Figure 8.1.4]])

27. **Weathering** breaks material down, while **erosion** removes and transports it. **Physical weathering** changes size while preserving composition; **chemical weathering** changes mineral composition and can form new minerals. (Course sections: [[03 - 8.2 Soil Erosion, Degradation, and Weathering Processes#Soil Erosion|8.2 Soil Erosion]] and [[03 - 8.2 Soil Erosion, Degradation, and Weathering Processes#Soil Weathering|8.2 Soil Weathering]])

28. Chemical fertilizer is mined or synthetically manufactured; organic fertilizer commonly comes from animal waste or composted plant material. Excessive use of either can alter soil and water chemistry and harm soils, plants, and animals. (Course section: [[03 - 8.2 Soil Erosion, Degradation, and Weathering Processes#Soil Erosion|8.2 Soil Erosion]])

29. **Porosity** is the amount or fraction of empty space in soil; **permeability** is how easily water moves through the soil. (Course section: [[04 - 8.3 Characterizing Soils and Soil Plasticity#Soil Texture|8.3 Soil Texture]])

30. The **dry-strength test** breaks dry soil by hand: sand-rich soil breaks easily and fine or clay-rich soil behaves more plastically. The **knife test** gives a shiny face for high clay and a crumbly face for low clay. The expert-only **teeth test** feels gritty with more silt and sand and smooth with mostly clay-sized particles. (Course section: [[04 - 8.3 Characterizing Soils and Soil Plasticity#Soil Texture|8.3 Soil Texture]])

31. The **liquid limit** separates liquid and plastic states; the **plastic limit** separates plastic and semi-solid states; the **shrinkage limit** separates semi-solid and solid states. (Course section: [[04 - 8.3 Characterizing Soils and Soil Plasticity#Soil Plasticity|8.3 Soil Plasticity]])
